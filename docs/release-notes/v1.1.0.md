Choose your document by name instead of looking up millimetres, control the
spacing and print quality, and save the sheet as an image as well as a PDF.

## New

**Pick your document by name.** Seventeen presets covering India, the United
States, the United Kingdom, Schengen, Canada, Australia, China, Japan,
Singapore, the UAE and New Zealand, plus stamp size, PAN card and generic ID
sizes. Search by country, by document, or by size — typing `35x45` works as well
as typing `india`.

Each preset carries the size **and** the background colour the authority asks
for, shown as a swatch so you can compare it against the wall before you shoot.
Sizes are a guide, and the picker says so: check with your embassy if you are
unsure.

**Space between photos** is now adjustable, from none to 10 mm in half-
millimetre steps. Dragging a photo snaps to whatever spacing you chose, so a
rearranged sheet still matches what "Reset to Grid" would produce.

**Print quality** — Standard, High or Maximum (300, 450 or 600 DPI). High is the
default. You are unlikely to see a difference on a normal print; the higher
settings are headroom for a sheet that is later enlarged or scanned.

**Save as an image**, not only a PDF. Choose when you tap Save / Share: PDF is
better for printing, an image is better for sending through a messaging app to
a print shop. The image is rendered from the same PDF, so it cannot disagree
with what would have printed.

**Optional border** around each photo, giving whoever cuts the sheet a line to
follow. Off by default. Switching it on never moves a photo or changes how many
fit — it is drawn inside each photo's own rectangle.

**How many fit** is now shown for the paper you have chosen, so you can see the
effect of changing size, paper or spacing.

## Fixed

**Nothing that shipped in 1.0.1 was broken by this release.** Two faults were
found and fixed in the new image export before it shipped:

- Rendering the image at print resolution asked the system for a single 139 MB
  allocation and ran the app out of memory. Image output is now capped at 8
  megapixels, which is far more than any screen or messaging app needs.
- The shared image was assembled in memory with a filename that pointed
  nowhere, leaving the receiving app with nothing to open. It is now written
  properly before being shared.

## Unchanged

Everything still happens on the device. No account, no upload, and the app still
forgets your photos when it closes.
